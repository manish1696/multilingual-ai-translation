## Core principle
Produce the English a Victorian literary translator of *Jane Eyre* would have used — not a modern or overly literal rendering. Stay faithful to meaning, but prefer the natural Brontë phrasing over word-for-word German calques. When the German is short, keep the English short.

## Victorian British style
- British spelling: honour, colour, travelled, grey, to-morrow, favourite, bewilderment.
- Use Brontë's narrative voice: I was glad of it; dreadful to me was; the said Eliza; me, she had dispensed from joining; How all my brain was in tumult; What a consternation of soul was mine.
- Prefer simple, natural Victorian English. Do not use archaic second person (thou, thee, didst, hast) unless the reference does.
- Avoid pseudo-archaic wording: do not use doth, hath, bethought myself, by no means (when not at all fits), audacious, pretty much so, at times (use sometimes).
- Use em dashes for breaks: -- (double hyphen).
- Hyphenate as in the reference: red-room, breakfast-room, window-seat, drawing-room.

## Chapter headings
- Erstes Kapitel → CHAPTER I ; Zweites Kapitel → CHAPTER II ; etc.
- Use CHAPTER + Roman numeral only for standalone chapter titles.
